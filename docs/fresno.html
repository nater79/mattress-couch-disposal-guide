How to get rid of a mattress or couch in Fresno, CA

Buying a new mattress? If a store delivers a new mattress to you in California, state law says the store must offer to pick up your old one at no extra cost. If it came by a shipping company, the store must offer to arrange pickup within 30 days. Stores may refuse a contaminated mattress (CalRecycle; Cal. Public Resources Code 42992, as summarized on that CalRecycle page).

City pickup and drop-off rules

One-Time Bulky Item Pickup

Picks up single bulky items too big for your carts, such as a couch, mattress, box spring, recliner or headboard, at no extra cost. You can use it once a year. It is only for homes that pay the City of Fresno for trash service, whose account is in good standing, and whose last bulky pickup was more than a year ago (One-Time Bulky Item Pickup).

How to book

To book, call 311 (559-621-2489 from outside the city) or use the FresGO app. Solid Waste will call you within 7 business days to set a date. Each home must ask for its own pickup.

More details

The city says piles nobody asked it to collect count as illegal dumping and can be cited.

Setting it out

Set it out no more than 36 hours before your date, and by 6:00 a.m. on the day. Place it 1 to 1.5 feet from the curb in front of your home, away from sewer vents, water meters, mailboxes and fire hydrants, 4 to 5 feet from speed bumps, and clear of parked cars.

Not taken, and second pickups

Not taken by this pickup: refrigerators and freezers, electronics, tires, batteries, household garbage and hazardous waste. A second pickup in the same year is a paid Special Haul (fee set in the city's Master Fee Schedule; call 559-621-6888 to ask or book, per the city's Residential Services page).

Operation Clean Up

Once a year, the city also picks up larger loads street by street. Mattresses, box springs and furniture are allowed. Find your estimated date with the lookup tool on the Operation Clean Up page or call 311.

More details

Set items out no earlier than 7 days before and by 6:00 a.m. on the day. The limit is 10 cubic yards (about 2 piles of 8 by 4 by 4 feet). Keep yard clippings in a separate pile. There is no alley pickup, so put piles at the curb in front of your home.

Apartments (4+ units)

Apartment buildings with 4 or more units are not part of Operation Clean Up. The city says to call your building's hauler: north of Ashlan Avenue, Allied Waste Services of Fresno (559-275-1551); south of Ashlan Avenue, Mid Valley Disposal (559-237-9425).

Free Dump Events

City trash customers can drop off bulky items, including mattresses and couches, at no extra cost at the Cedar Avenue Recycling and Transfer Station (CARTS), 3457 S. Cedar Ave. The last Fall 2026 event the city lists is November 14 and 15, 7:00 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. The limit is one full-size pickup truck load per household (about 8 by 4 by 4 feet). Bring a City of Fresno utility bill (or the city's Proof of Eligibility Form) and a state-issued ID.

Questions

Not sure if your item or home qualifies? Check with the city by calling 311 (559-621-2489).

Still in good shape? Donate it

If your couch or mattress is clean and in good shape, giving it away helps a neighbor and keeps it out of the landfill.

  • Offer it free to neighbors (Buy Nothing groups, Nextdoor, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist "free").
  • Call a local thrift store, church, shelter, or furniture bank. Ask if they take it and if they pick up.
  • Call first. Many charities do not take used mattresses.
  • If a store delivers a new mattress to you in California, state law says the store must offer to pick up your old one at no extra cost. If it came by a shipping company, the store must offer to arrange pickup within 30 days. Stores may refuse a contaminated mattress (CalRecycle; Cal. Public Resources Code 42992, as summarized on that CalRecycle page). You can also drop off a mattress for free at Bye Bye Mattress sites: byebyemattress.com/california.

Hiring a hauler instead

If the city cannot help, you can hire a licensed hauler. Ask for a written price first. Never pay someone who might dump it illegally.
