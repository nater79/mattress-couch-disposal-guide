City pickup and drop-off rules

No city bulky pickup

In Colorado Springs, trash is collected in an open market: residents and businesses sign up directly with private haulers for trash and recycling. This is true for single-family homes, apartments, and businesses (Waste Diversion Action Plan, December 2025). The city pages we checked do not list a city curbside pickup for mattresses or couches.

Ask your hauler

Ask your trash hauler whether it takes a mattress or couch, how to book it, and what it costs. Residents told the city that hauler guidelines are inconsistent (WastelessCOS), so check with your own hauler before you put anything at the curb. If you rent, ask your landlord or property manager who your hauler is.

Until it is picked up

City code says appliances, furniture, manufactured goods, and personal items must be stored inside an enclosed building or out of view in a rear yard. Garbage and household waste must be stored in a clean and sanitary manner (City Code: What you need to know).

Neighborhood cleanups

The city's Code Enforcement team invites neighbors to organize a free community cleanup. The city provides trash bags and trash removal after the cleanup is done; you gather neighbors, pick a spot, and nominate your neighborhood with the form on the Neighborhood Cleanup Program page. That page does not say whether mattresses or couches are taken, so check with the city before you plan around it.

Haul it yourself

The city says that, except for the household hazardous waste facility run by El Paso County, all waste processing facilities in the Colorado Springs area are privately owned and operated (Waste Diversion Action Plan). El Paso County's Recycling Directory has a Mattresses & Box Springs section and a Landfills & Transfer Stations section. Call to verify acceptance and fees before you go.

Questions and reports

To report trash or another City Code problem, contact Neighborhood Services at 719-444-7891, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Monday to Friday, at 30 S Nevada Ave., or file a City Code complaint online (Neighborhood Services). For pickup questions, check with your hauler first.

Anything not listed here: check with the city. We only list rules we could confirm on an official page.

Other options

Donate it

If your couch or mattress is clean and in good shape, giving it away helps a neighbor and keeps it out of the landfill.

  • Offer it free to neighbors (Buy Nothing groups, Nextdoor, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist "free").
  • Call a local thrift store, church, shelter, or furniture bank. Ask if they take it and if they pick up.
  • Call first. Many charities do not take used mattresses.

Hire a hauler

If the city cannot help, you can hire a licensed hauler. Ask for a written price first. Never pay someone who might dump it illegally.
