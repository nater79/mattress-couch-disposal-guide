How to get rid of a mattress or couch in Detroit, MI

Step 1: Can someone else use it? Donate first

If your couch or mattress is clean and in good shape, giving it away helps a neighbor and keeps it out of the landfill.

  • Offer it free to neighbors (Buy Nothing groups, Nextdoor, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist "free").
  • Call a local thrift store, church, shelter, or furniture bank. Ask if they take it and if they pick up.
  • Call first. Many charities do not take used mattresses.

Step 2: City pickup and drop-off rules

  • Bulk items are picked up at the curb on your trash day. The city's DPW page and its 2026 curbside flyer say bulk is now picked up weekly. An older city page still says every other week, so check your date with the pickup schedule tool.
  • The city lists furniture and mattresses as bulk items (City of Detroit, Free Dumping for Detroiters).
  • Limit: up to 2 cubic yards (about the size of 2 stoves) and no more than 1,000 pounds per pickup. Spread extra items over more weeks.
  • Put items out by 7 a.m. on collection day, and no earlier than 6 p.m. the day before. Put them in front of your own home, never in front of a vacant lot or vacant house.
  • Size note: one city page says all bulk items should be under 4 feet and 50 pounds, but the 2026 flyer gives that limit for renovation materials. If your couch is large, call DPW first at 313-876-0004.
  • Free drop-off for Detroit residents (bring a driver's license, state ID or Detroit ID): Southfield Yard (12255 Southfield), Davison Yard (8221 W. Davison) and J. Fons Transfer Station (6451 E. McNichols), up to 2 cubic yards or 1,000 pounds free. Hours change by season; see the 2026 flyer.
  • Too much for the curb? The city offers paid bulk pickup at $40 per cubic yard. Call 313-876-0004.
  • Get pickup reminders by texting your street address to 313-800-7905.

Anything not listed here: check with the city. We only list rules we could confirm on an official page.

Step 3: Other options

If the city cannot help, you can hire a licensed hauler. Ask for a written price first. Never pay someone who might dump it illegally.
