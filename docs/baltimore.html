City pickup and drop-off rules

Free curb pickup

the city says residents can put out up to 3 bulk items for trash collection each month. Sofas, chairs and other furniture, and mattresses and box springs, are on the city's list of items it picks up (Baltimore DPW).

Bulk pickup happens on a set Saturday each month. Call 311 or use 311 Online to find your bulk trash day. Have your address, your phone number and the 3 items ready, and call at least 4 working days before your Saturday so you get on the schedule.

Mark each item "bulk collection" and set it where your regular trash is picked up, by 7:00 a.m. on pickup day. If you have rear (alley or yard) pickup, leave items at the end of the yard and leave the gate unlocked.

Not picked up at the curb

building materials (like sheetrock, siding, wood pieces or roofing), auto parts, paint or stain, and flammable or hazardous waste.

Free drop-off

the city says its Residential Drop-Off Centers take furniture and other bulk items at no cost for Baltimore City residents with a valid ID (drop-off guide). Locations from the city's list: Eastern, 6101 Bowley's Lane (410-396-9950, Monday to Saturday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.); Sisson Street, 2840 Sisson St. (410-396-7250, Monday to Saturday 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.); Northwest Transfer Station, 5030 Reisterstown Road (410-396-2706, Monday to Saturday 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.); and Western, 701 Reedbird Ave. (410-396-3367, Saturdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. only while it is being modernized). Hours can change, so check the city's list before you go.

Drop-off limits

no more than 2 loads a day and 5 loads a week, and only in a passenger car, an unmodified van or minivan, or an unmodified pickup no larger than 3/4 ton. Stay with your vehicle.

The Quarantine Road Landfill (6100 Quarantine Road) charges $135 per ton for non-hazardous solid waste, so for a free couch or mattress drop-off use one of the centers above. If you are not sure, check with the city by calling 311.

Hiring a small hauler instead? The city says to ask the hauler where they will take your items, to help prevent illegal dumping.

Anything not listed here: check with the city. We only list rules we could confirm on an official page.

Still in good shape? Donate it

If your couch or mattress is clean and in good shape, giving it away helps a neighbor and keeps it out of the landfill.

  • Offer it free to neighbors (Buy Nothing groups, Nextdoor, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist "free").
  • Call a local thrift store, church, shelter, or furniture bank. Ask if they take it and if they pick up.
  • Call first. Many charities do not take used mattresses.

Hiring a hauler instead

If the city cannot help, you can hire a licensed hauler. Ask for a written price first. Never pay someone who might dump it illegally.
